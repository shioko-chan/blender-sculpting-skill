# Form review and stage gates

These prompts guide visual review; they are not automatic scores. Compare against the brief and reference, including any intentional stylization. Use the same lens, framing, lighting, and material when comparing revisions.

| Stage | Accept when | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blockout | At thumbnail size, the pose, balance, head/body ratio, large masses, and negative spaces communicate the intended subject. Front, profile, three-quarter, and back silhouettes agree about volume. | Change scale, placement, tilt, or depth of major masses; avoid surface detail. |
| Primary | Important skeletal landmarks and overlaps explain the visible form. Eyes belong to the orbital structure; jaw/muzzle has a clear underside and side plane; joints connect plausibly. Creature anatomy supports its pose and motion. | Return to mass relationships or landmarks, then rerender. |
| Secondary | Features, hair/clothing masses, folds, and plane breaks support the design in every useful view; no detail disguises an unresolved proportion problem. | Simplify or reposition the feature, or return to the primary stage. |
| Final | Chosen detail scale suits the intended framing. Surface marks follow form or compression. No prominent view has accidental intersections, flattened depth, broken silhouette, or distracting asymmetry. Deliverable files reopen and render. | Fix the named issue and make another checkpoint; do not label the result final by file name alone. |

## Four-view checklist

- **Front:** overall width ratios, centerline, shoulder/pelvis balance, paired landmark heights, planned asymmetry.
- **Profile:** cranial versus face/muzzle depth, brow–nose–chin rhythm, jaw angle, rib cage/pelvis depth, foot support.
- **Three-quarter:** transition between front and side planes, eye socket depth, cheek to jaw turn, overlap and contact shadows, volume of hair/horns/clothes.
- **Back:** cranium and neck connection, scapula/shoulder/pelvis relationships, mass taper, hair or costume silhouette.

For a creature, inspect at least one view that exposes its characteristic appendages and one that tests plausible weight bearing. For a hero prop or bust, adapt the checklist to the requested display angles.

Review in plain clay with neutral lighting before judging texture or color. Also try a small thumbnail or flat silhouette. Perspective can hide shape errors, so compare an orthographic view with the intended final camera. State one highest-impact correction per pass and document whether it helped in a new render. Numeric mesh counts and symmetry checks are supporting signals only.
